## 1. What EduLens Is

**EduLens** is an open student learning intelligence and performance analytics platform.

- **EduLens is NOT an AI tutor.** It does not teach students or answer tutoring questions directly.
- Instead, students learn using external LLMs (such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini).
- Students submit their chat logs to EduLens, which extracts quantifiable, interpretable conversational evidence to evaluate demonstrated understanding, detect learning struggles, and summarize curriculum mastery by topic.

---

## 2. Prototype Objectives Delivered

A robust, explainable Machine Learning prediction pipeline and interactive dashboard that:
1. Ingests raw student-LLM learning conversations in varied schemas.
2. Extracts verified, leakage-free conversational NLP features.
3. Trains **one primary interpretable ML model** to predict the student's demonstrated understanding level for the topic discussed.
4. Provides real-time inference on sample or custom learning sessions.
5. Generates a curriculum-level topic-wise performance summary across computer science topics.
6. Presents all results cleanly through a 3-page Streamlit dashboard.
7. Includes automated test coverage (`pytest`) for data loading, feature engineering, and inference.

## 4. Datasets Available in the Repository

The repository contains three primary dataset sources in `data/raw/`:

1. **Education Dialogue Dataset** (`data/raw/education_dialogue/`):
   - 47,234 conversations (40,000 train, 7,234 eval).
   - Generated by Gemini Ultra (Multi-turn RLHF paper, arXiv:2405.14655).
   - Covers school-level subjects (History, Biology, etc.).
   - *Status:* Unlabeled for supervised understanding tasks; preserved for unsupervised exploration.
2. **MathDial Dataset** (`data/raw/mathdial/`):
   - 2,861 math tutoring conversations (2,262 train, 599 test).
   - Human-annotated dialogue on GSM8K math word problems (EPFL).
   - *Status:* Measures arithmetic problem correction (`self-correctness`), not general topic understanding.
3. **Claude-Generated EduLens Datasets**:
   - `edulens_chats_dataset.json` (430 sessions across 11 CS subjects with verified topics, personas, and understanding labels). **Primary Supervised Dataset.**
   - `edulens_chats_set1 (1).json` (420 sessions).
   - `edulens_chats_set2 (1).json` (430 sessions).
   - `edulens_chats_set3 (1).json` (440 sessions).

## 6. The Chosen ML Prediction Target

**Target:** `final_understanding` (3-class Categorical Classification)
- Class 1: `understood` (Mastery / High comprehension) — 44.7% (192 sessions)
- Class 2: `partial` (Emerging / Incomplete comprehension) — 36.3% (156 sessions)
- Class 3: `confused` (Struggling / Needs intervention) — 19.1% (82 sessions)

---

## 7. Why That Target Was Selected

1. **Pedagogical Relevance:** In an educational diagnostics platform, categorization into *Understood*, *Partial*, or *Confused* matches Bloom's taxonomy progression and informs concrete teacher interventions.
2. **Ground Truth Validity:** `final_understanding` is directly observed in `edulens_chats_dataset.json`. Converting categories to arbitrary numerical percentages or predicting synthetic multiple-choice quiz scores would fabricate false precision.
3. **Inference Realism:** When analyzing a learning conversation in real time, the student has not yet taken a quiz. Estimating the demonstrated understanding level directly from the conversation provides immediate formative feedback.

---

## 8. Preprocessing and NLP Strategy

To prevent data leakage:
- Preprocessing is fitted **strictly on training data** inside a Scikit-learn `Pipeline`.
- **Target Leakage Safeguard:** `overall_sentiment` is 100% correlated with `final_understanding` in the synthetic generator and is strictly **excluded** from features.
- Inputs are derived exclusively from student utterances (`student_message`). Teacher/LLM responses are excluded from model features so the classifier evaluates only student evidence.

---

## 9. The Feature Set (108 Total Features)

Implemented in [`ml_prototype/features.py`](ml_prototype/features.py):

### A. Dense Conversational Structural Signals (8 Features, Scaled via StandardScaler)
1. `num_turns`: Total count of student utterances in the session.
2. `total_words`: Total word count typed by the student.
3. `avg_turn_length`: Average words per turn (struggling students often exhibit fragmented, short turns).
4. `question_count`: Frequency of question marks (`?`).
5. `confusion_markers`: Regex occurrences of confusion vocabulary (`confused`, `lost`, `stuck`, `don't understand`, `doubt`, etc.).
6. `clarification_markers`: Regex occurrences of clarification requests (`explain again`, `can you show`, `example`, etc.).
7. `understanding_markers`: Regex occurrences of comprehension affirmations (`makes sense`, `got it`, `understood`, `now I get`, `clear now`, etc.).
8. `code_indicators`: Count of code syntax elements (backticks, brackets `{}`, `def `, `return`, etc.).

### B. Lexical & Conceptual Vocabulary (100 Features)
- `TfidfVectorizer(max_features=100, stop_words="english", ngram_range=(1, 2), min_df=2)` fit on student text. Captures domain keywords versus hesitation markers.

## 10. The Model and Why It Was Selected

**Primary Model:** **Multinomial Logistic Regression** with balanced class weighting:
```python
LogisticRegression(class_weight="balanced", max_iter=1000, random_state=42, C=1.0)
```

**Why Selected:**
1. **Explainability & Transparency:** High educational value for student defense/viva. Model outputs well-calibrated class probabilities ($P(\text{understood})$, $P(\text{partial})$, $P(\text{confused})$).
2. **Feature Coefficient Interpretability:** Linear coefficients can be directly inspected to see which words and signals drove the prediction.
3. **No Hyper-Complexity:** Avoids deep neural nets or unneeded sentence transformers where a robust linear classifier achieves high discriminative power on verified conversational features without overfitting.

---

## 11. Training and Evaluation Methodology

- **Split:** Stratified 80% train (344 sessions) / 20% test (86 sessions), random seed 42.
- **Baseline:** `DummyClassifier(strategy="most_frequent")` (majority class baseline).
- **Validation:** 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation on the training set.
- **Verified Test Metrics (Held-Out Test Set):**
  - **Primary Model Test Accuracy:** `96.51%` (Baseline: `45.35%`)
  - **Macro Precision:** `0.9708`
  - **Macro Recall:** `0.9476`
  - **Macro F1-Score:** `0.9577` (Baseline: `0.2080`)
  - **Weighted F1-Score:** `0.9647`
  - **5-Fold CV Accuracy:** `91.88% (+/- 4.26%)`
  - **5-Fold CV Macro F1:** `90.31% (+/- 5.06%)`
- **Confusion Matrix on Held-Out Test Set:**
  ```
               Predicted: confused  partial  understood
  True: confused          14        2           0
  True: partial            0       30           1
  True: understood         0        0          39
  ```

## 14. Viva & Technical Defense Questions & Answers

### Q1: Why not just use an LLM prompt to classify the student's understanding?
> **Answer:** LLMs are nondeterministic, expensive at scale, slow for batch analytics, and susceptible to prompt drift and hallucinations. A scikit-learn classifier executes in less than 2 milliseconds per session, provides deterministic outputs with calibrated probabilities, and allows exact auditing of feature weights. EduLens reserves LLMs for where generative capacity is truly needed (e.g. generating custom quiz questions).

### Q2: How did you ensure there was no data leakage during feature extraction?
> **Answer:** All feature extraction logic (both TF-IDF n-grams and conversational metric scaling) is encapsulated in a Scikit-learn `Pipeline` and `ColumnTransformer`. The transformers are fitted strictly on the 80% training split (`X_train`) and only transform the 20% test split (`X_test`). Furthermore, synthetic metadata fields (like `overall_sentiment` or `quiz` score) were explicitly excluded from the feature matrix.

### Q3: Why Multinomial Logistic Regression instead of Random Forest or Neural Networks?
> **Answer:** In educational assessment, model interpretability and explainability are vital. Logistic Regression with balanced weighting yields transparent linear coefficients for every feature, calibrated probability distributions, and achieves 96.51% test accuracy without the risk of overfitting present in complex deep architectures on small-to-medium corpora.

### Q4: How is teacher/AI response handled during feature extraction?
> **Answer:** The feature extractor isolates student utterances only. The teacher's or tutor LLM's explanations are excluded from feature extraction so the model evaluates *only* what the student demonstrated and articulated.

## 15. Current Limitations and Known Issues

1. **Synthetic Nature of Primary Dataset:** The 430 training sessions were generated using Claude with pedagogical persona prompts. While realistic, they simulate student struggles rather than recording live classroom interactions.
2. **CS Domain Concentration:** The primary dataset focuses on university Computer Science subjects (DSA, DAA, OS, DBMS, ML, C++, Python). Generalization to humanities or non-STEM subjects has not been evaluated.
3. **No Student IDs over Time:** The dataset consists of independent sessions. Topic-wise aggregation currently reflects topic difficulty across sessions, not the longitudinal progression of a single user.
